Dynamic Sky

The DynamicSkyEffect renders the sky of a camera in place of the sky of the scene: a day sky of an analytic atmosphere lit by the sun, a night sky of stars and the moon, and optionally clouds drifting with the wind. The position of the sun sets the time of day, and the effect can keep a directional light in sync with it. It also generates the lighting of the scene from its sky, and can haze the scene with it.

The dynamic sky is not a built-in effect of the camera frame: create it, and add it to a camera frame with addEffect:

const sky = new DynamicSkyEffect(app.graphicsDevice);
sky.light = sunEntity.light;
sky.cloudType = CLOUDTYPE_VOLUMETRIC;
cameraFrame.addEffect(sky);
cameraFrame.update();

In the Editor, its script adds it, see The dynamic sky in the Editor.

Like all effects, its settings apply on the next CameraFrame#update. A day and night cycle moving the sun calls it every frame:

let time = 0;
app.on('update', (dt) => {
    time += dt;
    sky.elevation = Math.sin(time * 0.1) * 60;
    cameraFrame.update();
});

The sun and the time of day​

The azimuth and the elevation of the sun, in degrees, set the time of day. An elevation below 0 places the sun below the horizon, which gives the night sky. rotation turns the whole sky around the vertical axis, so the sky, the sun and the moon turn together.

The effect keeps the directional light assigned to its light in sync with the sky: its direction, color and intensity, and so the shadows it casts. By day the light shines from the sun at sunIntensity, dimmed by the clouds, and at night it fades to the moonlight, at moonIntensity and in the moonColor. The sky scales with sunIntensity too, so it sets the brightness of the whole day, to match the exposure of the camera.

The day sky​

PropertyDescription
turbidityThe haziness of the atmosphere, 1-20. Higher values give a milkier sky and a larger glow around the sun.
rayleighThe amount of the Rayleigh scattering, 0-4, setting the blueness of the sky.
mieCoefficientThe amount of the Mie scattering, 0-0.1, setting the brightness of the glow around the sun.
mieDirectionalGHow tightly the glow hugs the sun, 0-1.
luminanceThe brightness of the sky, relative to the light of the sun. At 1, the sky lights the scene as a clear sky does, so a single exposure suits the sky and the sunlit ground.
twilightGlowThe strength of the warm glow at the horizon while the sun is just below it.

The night sky​

PropertyDescription
nightColor, nightBrightnessThe color and the brightness of the night sky.
starBrightnessThe brightness of the stars. 0 disables them.
starDensityThe fraction of the cells of the sky holding a star, 0-1.
starSizeThe scale of the size of the stars.
moonDirectionThe direction toward the moon, turned by the rotation of the sky.
moonSizeThe angular radius of the moon disc, in degrees.
moonGlowThe brightness of the moon disc.

Clouds​

The sky has no clouds by default. Set cloudType to add them:

  • CLOUDTYPE_FLAT: a single flat layer of noise with simple shading, which is cheap. The sky shader evaluates them for each pixel, like the rest of the sky, so they also work in XR.
  • CLOUDTYPE_VOLUMETRIC: volumetric clouds, ray marched through a layer of 3D noise. They are the expensive part of the sky, and render to a texture of their own, at a reduced resolution. Their noise is generated on the CPU when they are first used.
PropertyDescription
cloudCoverageThe fraction of the sky covered by clouds, 0-1, from a clear sky to overcast.
cloudSoftnessThe softness of the clouds, 0.01-1, from dense clouds with crisp edges to translucent, wispy ones.
cloudOpacityThe opacity of the clouds, 0-1.
cloudBrightnessThe brightness of the sunlight falling on the clouds.
cloudNightBrightnessThe brightness of the moonlight on the clouds at night.
cloudSizeThe distance in meters over which the cloud pattern repeats. Larger values give larger clouds.
cloudHeightThe altitude of the cloud layer, in meters.
cloudThicknessThe thickness of the layer of the volumetric clouds, in meters.
cloudSunDimmingThe fraction of the sunlight the clouds block at the full coverage, dimming the light.
windSpeed, windDirectionThe speed the clouds drift with, in meters per second, and the direction they drift toward, in degrees. The clouds drift with the time of the application.

Two properties set the cost of the volumetric clouds:

  • cloudQuality, 0-1, sets the number of their steps through the cloud layer and toward the light. The cost grows about linearly, doubling from 0.5 to 1. Lower values give less defined edges and a flatter shading.
  • cloudResolution, 0.25-1, is the resolution the clouds render at, relative to the scene. The clouds are soft, so a reduced resolution saves most of their cost with little loss of detail.

Lighting the scene​

While its lighting is enabled, the effect generates the lighting of the scene from its sky: the environment atlas lighting the materials, assigned to Scene#envAtlas. Only one effect should do so, the one of the main camera.

Generating the lighting has a cost, so the effect generates it again only when the sky has changed enough:

  • lightingThreshold is the angle the sun moves by before the lighting is generated again.
  • lightingCloudThreshold is the distance the clouds drift by, as a fraction of their size, before it is generated again. 0, the default, disables it.
  • lightingInterpolation blends the lighting from its previous update to its latest one, so it follows a gradually changing sky smoothly, instead of changing at once.
  • bakeLighting generates the lighting again before the next frame, for a change it does not regenerate for on its own.

lightingResolution is the resolution of the sky the lighting is generated from, and atlasSize the size of the environment atlas. A larger atlas gives sharper reflections on glossy surfaces.

The cameras without the effect, such as the cameras rendering planar reflections, render the sky of the scene from that environment atlas: a still image of this sky.

Haze​

While its haze is enabled, the effect adds aerial perspective to the scene its camera renders: with their distance, surfaces fade into the color of the sky behind them. The haze is warm toward a low sun, blue away from it, and follows the gradient of the night sky after the sunset. It is densest at its base height and thins out with the height above it, so the valleys stay hazy while the view from high up is clear.

sky.haze = true;
sky.hazeDensity = 0.0002;
sky.hazeHeight = 300;
sky.hazeBaseHeight = 0;
cameraFrame.update();
PropertyDescription
hazeDensityThe density of the haze at its base height, per world unit: the fraction of the light it scatters over a unit of distance. A level view at the base height is 95% hidden at a distance of about 3 / density.
hazeHeightThe height over which the density falls to about a third, in world units. Lower values keep the haze closer to the ground.
hazeBaseHeightThe height the haze is densest at, in world units: usually the lowest ground of the scene, such as the level of the sea. Below it, the haze grows denser still.

Keep the density low. The haze takes the color of the sky near the horizon, which is much brighter than the sunlit ground, so even a few percent of haze brightens the distant surfaces noticeably.

The haze takes the place of the fog of the camera, Scene#fog or CameraComponent#fog: while it is enabled, the effect gives its camera a fog of its own, and gives the camera its fog back once the haze is disabled. The other cameras keep their fog.

  • The lit materials, such as StandardMaterial, are hazed.
  • The particles and the gaussian splats render without fog on the camera.
  • A material opts out of the haze, and renders without fog, with useFog set to false, or with the DYNAMIC_SKY_NO_HAZE define:
material.setDefine('DYNAMIC_SKY_NO_HAZE', true);
material.update();

Enabling or disabling the haze compiles the shaders of the lit materials the camera renders again, so set it up front rather than toggling it during the game.

The effect hazes the materials with a replacement of the fogPS shader chunk, for all the materials of the graphics device. A material replacing fogPS with a chunk of its own is not hazed, and finds the FOG define of its shaders set to DYNAMICSKYHAZE on the camera.

The sky in other shaders​

The effect sets the uniforms of its sky on the graphics device every frame, so the shaders of other materials can evaluate the same sky, for example a water shader reflecting it. Its shader chunks, in GLSL and WGSL, evaluate the sky from these uniforms:

  • dynamicSkyAtmospherePS: dynamicSkyBase(dir, ambient) returns the radiance of the sky in a direction, without the sun disc, the stars and the moon, and sets ambient to the light of the sky around it. The direction is in the space of the sky: a world space direction turned by the rotation of the sky, the dynSkyRotation uniform, with X flipped.
  • dynamicSkyHazePS: dynamicSkyHaze(color, cameraPos, worldPos) returns a linear color seen through the haze, for the shaders the haze does not reach on its own.
  • GLSL
  • WGSL
#include "dynamicSkyAtmospherePS"
uniform mat3 dynSkyRotation;

vec3 skyColor(vec3 worldDir) {
    vec3 dir = worldDir * dynSkyRotation;
    dir.x = -dir.x;
    vec3 ambient;
    return dynamicSkyBase(dir, ambient);
}
#include "dynamicSkyAtmospherePS"
uniform dynSkyRotation: mat3x3f;

fn skyColor(worldDir: vec3f) -> vec3f {
    var dir = worldDir * uniform.dynSkyRotation;
    dir.x = -dir.x;
    var ambient: vec3f;
    return dynamicSkyBase(dir, &ambient);
}

On the CPU, getSkyColor evaluates the same sky in a direction, for lighting worked out in a script, such as the light the sky gives to particles:

const zenith = sky.getSkyColor(Vec3.UP);

With more than one dynamic sky, a material renders with the sky that was updated last.

XR​

In XR, the sky renders for each view, like the rest of the scene, and so do the flat clouds. The volumetric clouds render to their texture once, for the view of the camera, and each view reads them from it. Where the view of an eye extends past the view of the camera, the texture has no clouds to read, and they streak at its edge. Use CLOUDTYPE_FLAT clouds in XR.

The dynamic sky in the Editor​

The dynamicSkyEffect script, from dynamic-sky.mjs, adds the effect to the camera frame of the camera entity it is on, next to the cameraFrame script. Its attributes are the properties of the effect, in the groups Sun, Day, Night, Clouds and Lighting. The Light attribute of the Sun group is the entity of the directional light kept in sync with the sun.

The script does not animate the time of day. It copies its attributes into the effect every frame, so a script of your own on the same entity moves the sun by changing them:

import { Script } from 'playcanvas';

class DayNightCycle extends Script {
    static scriptName = 'dayNightCycle';

    time = 0;

    update(dt) {
        this.time += dt;
        const sky = this.entity.script.dynamicSkyEffect;
        sky.sun.elevation = Math.sin(this.time * 0.1) * 60;
    }
}

export { DayNightCycle };

The haze is not one of the attributes of the script. Set it on the effect the script drives, its effect, which the script creates in its initialize:

import { Script } from 'playcanvas';

class SkyHaze extends Script {
    static scriptName = 'skyHaze';

    postInitialize() {
        const sky = this.entity.script.dynamicSkyEffect.effect;
        sky.haze = true;
        sky.hazeDensity = 0.0002;
        sky.hazeHeight = 300;
    }
}

export { SkyHaze };

Limitations​

  • The volumetric clouds are not supported in XR, see XR.
  • The sky does not work together with the volumetric fog of the camera frame: the clouds cast no shadows into the fog, and the fog is not lit by the light of the sky.
  • The haze does not reach the particles and the gaussian splats, which render without fog on the camera with the haze.
